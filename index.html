Before anybody sells you SEO, three things on your site have to be fixed — and two of them are costing you calls right now.

Your phone button does not work on mobile. Your homepage is around 20 MB. There is no analytics, no conversion tracking and no pixel, so nothing anyone does for you can be proved.

A homeowner standing in their yard after a storm, searching on their phone, does not see your call button. Every dollar of SEO or advertising spent before this is fixed is spent sending people to a page that will not let them ring you.

Finding 2 — Nothing On The Site Can Be Measured
Google Analytics (G-): none Google Tag Manager: none Google Ads tag (AW-): none Meta pixel: none Google Ads Transparency Center, budgetroofingllcaz.com: "No ads found" Meta Ad Library, your page: no live ads
Why this matters more than the SEO quote

An agency charging $1,600 a month for SEO on this site cannot show you a single booked job that came from their work, because nothing counts calls or forms. Neither could we. Measurement goes on first — it takes twenty minutes and it costs nothing.

That is also the honest answer to being burnt by Angi, Thumbtack and Yelp. You paid for leads you could not trace and did not own. The way out is a site that counts every call and every form, so you can see what a booked job actually costs.

Finding 3 — The Homepage Is Around 20 MB
Homepage weight: ~20 MB across 37 images Biggest single image: 4.65 MB job photo A raw screenshot file: 3.1 MB (Screenshot-2026-05-28-103144.png) A logo image loaded twice: 2.35 MB each time Images set to load lazily: 5 of 37
A normal homepage is under 2 MB

On a phone in the East Valley, that page can take many seconds to appear — and a lot of people leave before it does. Google also uses page speed when deciding who to show. Resizing the images is an afternoon's work and it makes every other thing we do work better.

Finding 4 — The Site Does Not Say Where You Work
Pages on budgetroofingllcaz.com: 9 Pages for a city you serve: 0 Lyons Roofing: 129 city pages Almeida Roofing: ~25 Right Way Roofing: 24 Overson Roofing: 21 Your page titles: "budgetroofingllc" · "Services - budgetroofingllc" no city, no service, and no written descriptions
This is the cheapest ground available to you

Somebody in Mesa, Chandler or Queen Creek searching "roof repair" is shown pages about Mesa, Chandler and Queen Creek. You have none. Every established competitor built these pages — they are not doing anything clever, they just did the work.

Your own Yelp listing already says you cover Gilbert, Phoenix, Mesa, Chandler, Tempe and Scottsdale. Your website only mentions Gilbert and the East Valley.

Finding 5 — Six Real Reviews, And Three That Are Not
On your site: "EXCELLENT - Based on 6 reviews" (Google, via your review widget) Also on your site, on /reviews/ and /services/: "Michael R." | "Sarah T." | "David L." shown with stock avatar images (avatar-1.webp, avatar-2.webp, avatar-3.webp) and no matching review we could find on any platform
Take these down before anything else is built

Whoever built the site filled the gaps with placeholder testimonials. In the US, published testimonials have to reflect real customers — the FTC enforces that, and a competitor or a customer can report it. You do not need them anyway.

You have six genuine Google reviews and two of them name you personally. Real names, real jobs in Gilbert. Those belong on the homepage instead.

Finding 6 — Contradictions A Customer Will Notice
Homepage: "FREE INSPECTIONS" /roof-inspection/: "Professional Roof Inspection - $150" About page counters: "0+ Roofs Completed" "0+ Years Experience" "0+ Happy Clients" Homepage services: Roof Repair, Roof Inspections, Roof Replacement, Gutter Installation Services page: ...but Gutter Installation is replaced by tile roofing Dead links (href="#"): 10 — including every service card Gallery page address: /gallary/ (and the heading reads "Gallary") Default WordPress post: /hello-world/ still live and findable on Google
"0+ Years Experience" is on your About page right now

Individually these are small. Together they tell a homeowner choosing between three roofers that nobody finished the job. The free-versus-$150 inspection contradiction is the one that costs money — it makes the first promise look untrue.

Finding 7 — Two Things You Have And Do Not Use
Both are proof, and both are missing from your site
  • You are listed as a GAF certified contractor on GAF's own contractor directory — and your website never mentions it. That is manufacturer backing your competitors put on their homepage.
  • You are BBB accredited with an A rating, accredited since June 2026. Also not on the site.
  • Your license number does not appear anywhere on the site, although the homepage says "Licensed, Bonded, Insured". Arizona law (A.R.S. § 32-1124(B)) requires the ROC number on published advertising, including websites. We have not checked the register, so this is about what the site shows, not about your license.

Why The Timing Matters
National Weather Service, Phoenix
"Our 'monsoon season' officially extends from June 15th through September 30th" Phoenix, 2024: 143 days at or above 100°F, 70 days at or above 110°F EPA: "standard black asphalt roofs can reach 165 to 185°F at midday during the summer" "The roof materials tend to crack and age prematurely from sun damage"

Monsoon season ends in two weeks. Storm damage from this summer is being discovered now, and heat damage is a year-round argument in Phoenix. The fixes above are days of work, not months.

What We Would Do, In Order
  • Turn the phone button back on for mobile and fix the dead links. Today.
  • Install measurement — analytics, call tracking and conversion tracking — so every call and form is counted from day one.
  • Take down the placeholder testimonials and put your six real Google reviews, your GAF listing and your BBB accreditation on the homepage. Add your license number.
  • Fix the contradictions — free or $150 inspections, the services lists, the 0+ counters, the gallery spelling.
  • Build a page for each city you serve — Gilbert, Mesa, Chandler, Queen Creek, Tempe, Scottsdale, Phoenix — using your own job photos from each one.
  • Then advertise, judged on cost per booked job, not clicks — and with reviews requested after every single job.

Fix what is free first. Then decide what to pay for.

The call button, the tracking and the fake testimonials cost nothing to put right.
